## Supported interpretation

- This sample contains one strong match, one reasonable reach, and three substantial hard-mismatch applications.
- It does not prove the Backend resume broadly fails.
- The GenTech rejection is the clearest signal that the Cloud resume should describe operations, Linux/networking, logs/incidents, backups/recovery, migration/rollback, security, and cost decisions—not only list AWS services and deployment automation.

- Applicant count does not reveal applicant quality, other recruiting channels, or shortlist size.

## Resume-message findings

The current resumes communicate the technology stack but compress away much of the engineering story:

- inherited/local-only or earlier-state context;
- the before/after transformation;
- why versioning/default governance was introduced;
- operational/cloud trade-offs and troubleshooting;
- independently verified calculations and guarded releases;
- rigorous AIRIS measurement and attribution boundaries;
- AnotherEdenAI's deterministic/LLM authority design and honest evaluation gates.

This supports a resume refresh based on evidence. It does not authorize stronger claims than the project registries.

## Targeting classification

- `A - strong fit`: roughly 70%+ alignment and no hard blocker. Use for meaningful response-rate diagnosis.
- `B - reasonable reach`: strong foundation with one major gap. Apply, but interpret rejection cautiously.
- `C - lottery/weak target`: multiple hard requirements missing, wrong core stack, working-language mismatch, or major seniority gap. Apply only when effort is low; do not use rejection to judge market value.

- A/B/C fit;

## Do not conclude

- A high raw rejection percentage equals a bad resume.
- All applications are equally diagnostic.
- A title such as `Backend Developer` or `Python Developer` establishes stack/seniority fit.
- Twenty-one visible LinkedIn applicants means only twenty-one people competed.
- One strong-match rejection proves Hydar is unqualified or cannot reach the RM6.0k-RM6.5k target.
