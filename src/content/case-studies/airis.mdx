## Summary

AIRIS is an enterprise retrieval-augmented-generation workload at the Aerospace Malaysia Innovation Centre. I worked primarily on applied-AI engineering experiments: measuring retrieval and LLM behavior, reducing unnecessary context, and tracing concurrency bottlenecks rather than changing the system based on intuition.

## Context

AI-assisted analysis workflows combine retrieval, model calls, and application services. A successful single request does not explain whether context is necessary or which subsystem dominates under concurrent demand, so I separated controlled observations from later recommendations.

## Controlled context experiment

I designed a controlled RAG comparison to measure whether large amounts of locally supplied context were necessary. Across a **6-scenario/10-turn controlled experiment**, the optimized approach reduced local context from **541k to 20.9k local-context tokens**, a **96.1% reduction**, while retaining **10/10 citation checks passed**.

Passing the experiment's citation checks does not prove that answer quality universally improved. The checks establish the result for the defined scenarios and turns; they do not support a general accuracy or production-quality claim.

## Concurrency and bottleneck diagnosis

Instead of treating increasing end-to-end latency as a generic AI-performance problem, I decomposed request time by subsystem. Vector-search latency grew from **4.8 seconds at 10 concurrent users** to **51.0 seconds at 50 concurrent users**; at 50 users, vector search represented **76.9% of request time**. The testing also surfaced outbound-LLM behavior as another optimization area.

These figures describe the observed test conditions, not a supported production concurrency level or a measured production improvement.

## Engineering outcome and attribution

I presented the findings and remediation options to the senior engineer. The recommendations were reviewed and accepted during engineering discussions, and several were subsequently incorporated into the senior engineer's implementation. I authored the testing and optimization analysis and supported stakeholder training; later implementation ownership remains explicitly attributed to the senior engineer.

## Scope and limitations

This case study does not claim ownership of the overall AIRIS architecture, implementation of every recommendation, a universal answer-quality improvement, supported production concurrency, or a resulting production-performance gain. It omits private documents and data, internal service or endpoint details, model and configuration details, dashboards, report figures, credentials, and copied professional artifacts.
